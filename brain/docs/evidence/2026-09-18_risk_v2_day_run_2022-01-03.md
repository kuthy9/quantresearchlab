## 2. Did the five fixes act?

1. **Thesis lifecycle — yes.** Nine thesis ids over the session; after the
   seven stop-outs the next order came 0 times within 15 minutes (14 of 23
   before), shared no object with the stopped plan (8 of 23 before), and
   went the same way twice (11 before). The book refused three
   re-expressions; the Brain kept DEVELOPING through the cooldown instead of
   renaming the thesis. Orders fell from 47 to 26, fills from 29 to 9.
2. **Invalidation on the thesis's scale — yes.** Every one of the 26 orders
   put the invalidation on the governing scale or one below (1H theses on
   15m objects, 15m theses on 15m or 5m); the median stop is 1.09 ATR(5m)
   against 0.60. `CLOSE_BEYOND` was chosen in 38 of 111 ACTIONABLE replies;
   both close-beyond exits fired on a 5m close beyond the object and
   flattened at the next open, with the hard stop untouched.
3. **A sizing veto no longer moves the stop — yes.** One `position_size`
   veto (5 before); in the 38 later calls that carried it the Brain never
   re-proposed the same entry with a nearer invalidation (once before).
4. **Leverage bound — partly.** 25 of 26 orders were sized at two contracts
   by the 8× cap; the 1.5 % budget never bound (a 10-point stop risks 400
   USD, 0.4 %). But the cap was checked per submission, so at 10:01 UTC two
   positions held four contracts (1.3 M notional, 13×). Fixed after the
   run: the cap now subtracts the contracts already open (§4).
5. **Re-reasoning on the thesis's scale — yes.** Calls fell 34 %; the 252
   calls the day before that fired on a 5m pool crossing price are gone (75
   relation-only calls remain, on 15m and 1H objects); the Brain asked to
   sleep three times, which it never did before. Coverage of the 81 sharp
   moves is unchanged (78; RTH 25 of 25).

What did not change: the direction. Proposals were SHORT for 68 of the
first 82 ACTIONABLE replies while the session drifted up, LONG at the
open's drop (14 of 17 in the 09:00 hour, drift −70) and flat-to-long
after the rally; six of the seven stop-outs ran ≥ 1 R further against the
trade within an hour. The Brain still reads the 4H "short expansion" as
governing (46 of 111 ACTIONABLE replies on 1H, 65 on 15m, none on 4H or
5m). The lifecycle stopped the churn; it does not pick the side.

## 3. The parameters

Judged on this run and on arithmetic at 100 000 USD:

- **1.5 % / 2 % per trade.** Nominal. With `max_leverage` 8 the account
  holds two NQ, and two contracts on the 5–25-point stops the Brain names
  risk 200–1 000 USD (0.2–1.0 %). The 1.5 % budget would need a 37-point
  stop on two contracts; it bound once (the 39-point close-beyond stop, one
  contract). `A_PLUS` was never proposed, so the 2 % tier was not exercised.
- **Three positions.** Two were open at once, once; with the leverage cap
  counting open contracts (§4) a 100 000 account holds two contracts in
  total, so "three positions" means three one-contract theses or fewer. The
  limit is not the binding one; leverage is.
- **Minimum 2 R / preferred 3 R.** 2 R bound 16 times (5 at 1.5 R before)
  — the floor now shapes what reaches the broker. 3 R only matters with
  `A_PLUS`, unexercised.
- **Daily stop 2.5 %.** Not reached: the session bottomed at −2.06 % on
  the fills. Eight losers of 170–500 USD each add up to it; on the previous
  run's behaviour (23 stops) it would have fired before 22:00 UTC. It is
  the parameter that will act most often at this size.
- **Hard stop 6.5 %.** Not reached (worst −2.4 % from the peak). At 200–500
  USD a loss it is 13–30 consecutive losers; it protects against a broken
  day, not a bad one.
- **TTL 15 bars.** 12 of 26 orders expired unfilled (10 of 47 before): with
  the entry now often a 15m object rather than the nearest 5m one, more
  limits wait for a touch that does not come. Not wrong; worth watching.

## 4. Defects the run exposed, fixed after it

- `VirtualAccount.apply_fill` kept the first fill's average price when a
  same-direction fill added contracts; the second position's exit was then
  realized against the wrong average (320 USD on T5/T6). Never reachable
  with one position. Now the contract-weighted average
  (`execution/tests/test_simulated_executor.py`).
- The leverage cap counted only the order at hand. Now
  `floor(equity × max_leverage ÷ notional) − contracts already open`
  (`risk/tests/test_gate.py`). Under it every order placed while T5's two
  contracts were open is a `LEVERAGE` veto, so the replay diverges at
  09:30 UTC (`trade veto where the journal has submitted`), the LLM inputs
  stop matching from there (135 of 343 calls reproduced), and the run is
  kept as evidence, not as the regression baseline
  (`regression_baselines.json` stays empty until the next complete run).
